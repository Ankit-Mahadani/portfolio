The brief here came from a real bottleneck: a sales team that needed to reach 500+ B2B leads a week in Hebrew, and no realistic amount of manual dialing was going to hit that number. So the agent had to do two things a script can't — read documents to answer real questions, and know when to pick up the phone instead of sending another text.

## Two channels, one brain

<Fig src="01-pipeline-diagram.png" alt="Pipeline diagram: Telegram trigger and Firecrawl scraper into Gemini agent and Supabase vector store, out to Telegram reply and Sheets log" caption="Two inputs, one reasoning core, two outputs — the whole agent." span="bleed" kind="diagram" />

Telegram carries the first touch and most of the back-and-forth; Twilio voice picks up leads that stop responding on text. Both routes feed the same Gemini 2.5 reasoning core, so a lead never gets a different personality depending on which channel they answered on.

## Answering from a live source

<Pull>A lead asking about pricing gets the current price, not whatever was true when the prompt was written.</Pull>

The knowledge base is the part that made this a real sales tool instead of a chatbot with a good opening line. Firecrawl keeps product and pricing pages current, Supabase pgvector stores the embeddings, and every reply is grounded in retrieval rather than memorized text — so when pricing changes, the agent's answers change with it, automatically.

## When text isn't enough

Some leads go quiet on Telegram but will pick up a phone. Twilio handles that escalation — a voice call in Hebrew, same qualification logic, logged the same way as a text conversation.

Most personal-assistant demos are chatbots that answer questions well and forget everything the moment you close the tab. This one doesn't get closed — it runs as a standing n8n workflow, watching a mailbox and a calendar the way a human assistant would, and it's still running the week after I stopped touching it.

## What it actually does

The brief for myself was narrow on purpose: don't build a chatbot, build the thing behind the chatbot. So the assistant reads incoming mail over IMAP, classifies intent with Gemini through LangChain, and takes one of a small set of real actions — draft a reply, create a calendar hold, send a reminder, or escalate to me directly when it isn't confident.

<Pull>Confidence gates every autonomous action — when the model isn't sure, it asks instead of guessing.</Pull>

That gate is the difference between a demo and something you can leave alone for a month. Early versions of the classifier were happy to auto-book meetings from ambiguous mail. The fix wasn't a better prompt — it was routing anything below a confidence threshold to a "needs review" queue instead of acting on it.

Five stages, and every one of them was a separate failure mode I had to design around — IMAP connections that silently drop, a calendar API that rate-limits under 100+ weekly writes, and reminders that need to fire once, not every time the workflow re-triggers on retry.

## What it's saved

Since it went live, it's handled 85% of routine admin without me touching it — replying to scheduling requests, setting reminders, and keeping the calendar current across 100+ events a week. The remaining 15% is mostly stuff that should stay manual: anything with money attached, or a person who sounds upset.

<Metric value="85" unit="%" label="of routine tasks handled with no human step" />

## What I'd change

The confidence gate works, but it's a single global threshold right now. The honest next step is per-category thresholds — a scheduling request and a "can we push the deadline" email shouldn't share the same bar for autonomous action.

This one started from a simple frustration: radiologists reading dozens of scans a day don't need another dashboard, they need a fast, boring second opinion that's right often enough to trust. So the whole app is one screen — upload a scan, get a classification and a confidence score.

## The model

DenseNet was the right backbone for this specifically because of how it reuses features across layers — useful when the differences between a benign shadow and a malignant one are subtle and low-contrast, which is most of the time in lung and pancreas imaging.

<Fig src="01-architecture.png" alt="DenseNet architecture diagram with preprocessing and classification stages labelled" caption="DenseNet backbone, three modalities, one output head." span="bleed" kind="diagram" />

<Pull>The hard part wasn't the model. It was making three imaging modalities look like the same input to it.</Pull>

CT, MRI, and X-ray scans don't share a scale, contrast profile, or noise pattern. Before any of them reach the DenseNet backbone, OpenCV normalizes intensity, crops to the region of interest, and resizes to a common input shape — the preprocessing pipeline is arguably where more of the accuracy actually came from than the model architecture itself.

<Compare before="02a-raw-scan.png" after="02-preprocessing.png" beforeAlt="Raw CT scan slice with scanner noise and inconsistent contrast" afterAlt="Preprocessed CT scan slice after OpenCV normalization" />

## Where it's right, and wrong

At 94% accuracy on held-out scans, the honest framing is a second reader, not a replacement — the 6% it misses cluster in a specific place: early-stage, low-contrast cases where even a second human read is genuinely hard.

<Fig src="03-confusion-matrix.png" alt="Confusion matrix for the classifier across benign, malignant, and unclear classes" caption="Where the 6% of misses cluster." span="half" kind="diagram" />

<Metric value="94" unit="%" label="accuracy across CT, MRI, and X-ray scans" />

## Shipping it as a tool

A model in a notebook doesn't help anyone, so the last stage was wrapping it in a Flask app simple enough that the interface itself can't introduce error: upload a scan, wait under a second, read a confidence score. No settings, no modes, nothing to misconfigure.
